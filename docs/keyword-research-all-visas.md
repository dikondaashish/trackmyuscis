## Ways to add more value than a blog post

These pages beat articles because they answer the search directly and bring people back. Every one leads to "Track Case".

1. **A processing-time page per form**, for example `/processing-times/i-130`, I-485, N-400, I-140, I-765, I-131, I-751, I-90, I-129 and I-129F. Each covers the official time for each category and office, the date we checked, what to do if you're past it, and a monthly history we save ourselves. This group has the biggest and fastest-rising searches we could rank for, and it fits the tracker exactly. PERM is on the Department of Labor's site (flag.dol.gov) and gets its own page.
2. **A Visa Bulletin hub and priority date checker.** A visitor picks their category and country and enters their priority date. The tool says whether that date is current under Final Action and Dates for Filing, with each month's official cutoff since 2023. It shows history only, never predictions. Monthly posts link into it. It targets visa bulletin (100K–1M) and the "eb2 india priority date" family (10K–100K).
3. **A citizenship test practice tool.** The official civics questions as flash cards and a practice test, plus the reading and writing vocabulary, in English and Spanish. It targets citizenship test and civics test (10K–100K) and examen de ciudadania (rising). Check which civics test version USCIS uses now before building.
4. **A hub page for each visa:** H-1B, L-1, O-1, TN, E-2, H-4, K-1, B-1/B-2, F-1, J-1, TPS, DACA, asylum, EB-1, EB-2 NIW, EB-3, EB-5 and the marriage green card. Each has the same short template: what it is, which forms, the official processing time, fees, the latest changes and our posts. Hubs give Google a clear cluster for each audience and give us internal links.
5. **A page for each case status message** (split out of /status-explained). Searches like "new card is being produced", "case was approved" and "fingerprint fee was received" get 100–1K each, and there are dozens of them. Every visitor to one of these pages has an active case.
6. **Spanish versions of the top pages:** estatus de caso, tiempos de procesamiento, boletín de visas, permiso de trabajo, examen de ciudadanía and renovar green card. Spanish searches are large and have little good competition.
7. **USCIS office pages:** the five service centers, the National Benefits Center and field offices. Cover what each one handles and its processing times. Search Console already shows office searches reaching us ("family petition north bergen, nj", "uscis msc service center").
8. **A fee page** with every form's current fee, online versus paper, linked to the official fee calculator. It targets "uscis fee calculator" (10K–100K), "n400 fee" (10K–100K) and "uscis fees".
9. **An I-94 guide.** Covers how to download it, read it, fix it, and what to do if it expired. The I-94 is a CBP record, but every visa holder needs it, and "i94" gets 100K–1M searches.

## What to write first (in order)

The rules stay the same: facts come only from official sources, checked on the day; no predictions; the cover image shows the topic; one post per day.

**Weeks 1–2: the tracker's core audience (people with a pending case)**
1. Processing-time pages: N-400, I-130, I-485, I-140 and I-765 first.
2. USCIS case status not changing? What each message means (status pages, item 5).
3. How to get your I-94.
4. How to change your address with USCIS (answer page exists; expand it into a guide).
5. USCIS fees by form in 2026.

**Weeks 3–4: H-1B and work visas (largest group of future subscribers)**
6. H-1B processing time and premium processing.
7. H-1B transfer: can you start work when it's filed?
8. H-1B extension and the 6-year limit, including AC21.
9. H-1B fees, including the $100,000 payment. Read the proclamation and USCIS guidance first.
10. H-1B layoff: the 60-day grace period and your options.
11. H-1B lottery: registration, selection and results. Check the current selection rule first.
12. L-1, O-1 and TN, one guide each, then a post on going from O-1 to a green card.

**Weeks 5–6: green card and family**
13. Marriage green card: steps, documents and timeline.
14. K-1 fiancé visa: process, cost and timeline.
15. I-751 removing conditions: when to file and the 48-month extension letter. Check the current receipt validity on uscis.gov.
16. Affidavit of support (I-864) and public charge, explained simply.
17. NVC and consular processing: from I-130 approval to the interview.
18. EB-2 NIW, EB-1A and PERM processing time.

**Weeks 7–8: citizenship, humanitarian, Spanish**
19. N-400: interview, test and oath, with the practice test tool.
20. TPS: which countries, the dates and EAD extensions. This topic changes often, so add an update date and a review rule.
21. DACA renewal: fees, timing and whether new applications are open.
22. Asylum (I-589): the clock, the work permit and the travel document.
23. Spanish: estatus de caso, boletín de visas, permiso de trabajo.

**Every month:** Visa Bulletin page about the 1st of the month before, filled in on release day.

## Before writing any of these

- H-1B $100,000 payment, the H-1B selection rule, the current civics test, DV-2027 dates and TPS country dates all change often. Read the current uscis.gov, state.gov or Federal Register page on the day you write, and cite it.
