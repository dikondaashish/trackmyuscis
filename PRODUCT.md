# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People in the United States waiting on a USCIS decision: green card applicants and their families, work-permit and travel-document applicants, workers on employer petitions, and green card holders applying for citizenship. Many are anxious, some read English as a second language, and most arrive on a phone from a Google search or an AI assistant with one question ("What does this status mean?", "How long does I-485 take?"). The job to be done: understand where their case stands and what usually happens next, in under a minute, without being misled or sold to.

## Product Purpose

TrackMyUSCIS explains a USCIS case in plain English: it decodes receipt numbers, explains every case status and the usual steps for each form, points to official processing times, answers common questions, and helps people find licensed legal help. Success means a visitor leaves calmer and correctly informed, finds the official source, and comes back when their status changes. It is independent: never affiliated with USCIS or the government, and never legal advice.

## Brand Personality

Quiet confidence. Calm, trustworthy, plain-spoken. It sounds like a knowledgeable friend who checked the official source, not a law firm, a government office or a growth-hacking startup. Confidence comes from accurate, sourced information, never from promises about outcomes. Emotional goal: relief and clarity.

## Anti-references

- Government-site imitation: seals, eagles, flags, "official" styling or anything implying USCIS affiliation.
- Lead-generation immigration sites: pop-ups, chat widgets that push consultations, countdowns, "guaranteed approval", fear-based copy.
- Crowded dashboards and metric walls; charts that look like real statistics when they are examples.
- Generic AI-landing-page look: gradient blobs, glassmorphism, emoji icons, eyebrow labels, slogans, "Learn more" buttons.
- Stock photos of distressed people, passports with emblems or government buildings.

## Design Principles

1. **One job per screen.** Each page answers one question or offers one main action; everything else becomes a quiet text link. Remove before you style.
2. **Answer first, then detail.** Lead with the direct answer or the current status; history, comparisons and edge cases come after.
3. **Honest by default.** Label examples as Examples, separate official information from our explanations, link the official source, and never claim a feature that isn't live.
4. **Plain words for anxious readers.** Short sentences, everyday vocabulary, verbs on buttons, no jargon without a definition, no exclamation marks.
5. **Calm, precise craft.** Generous space, one type scale, one card style, restrained colour and motion: the polish of Stripe and Linear with the clarity of GOV.UK.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA throughout: keyboard access and visible focus on everything, 4.5:1 text contrast, 44px minimum touch targets, labels on every field, layouts that work at 320px wide and 200% zoom, and reduced-motion support. Write for readers with limited English and high stress: short sentences, defined terms, no colour-only meaning. Many users are on older phones and slow connections, so pages stay light and fast.
